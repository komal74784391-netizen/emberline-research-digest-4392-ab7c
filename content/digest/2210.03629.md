ReAct interleaves natural-language reasoning traces with task-specific actions so that a language model can plan, act, observe the result and revise its plan. Reasoning traces help the model keep track of goals and avoid repeating mistakes, while actions let it gather fresh evidence from a search engine or an interactive environment. The paper evaluates the combination on question answering and on text-based games, where it outperforms both reasoning-only and acting-only baselines and produces more interpretable behaviour. The approach remains a foundation for modern tool-using agents.
